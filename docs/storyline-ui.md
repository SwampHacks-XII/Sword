# Storyline UI

The reference image guides the whole in-app theme: pastel pixel sky and meadow,
warm grid-textured parchment, muted purple swords, and a little green forest NPC
at the lower-left edge of the scroll. The guide is currently named **Moss**.

## Player journey

1. **Welcome:** animated meadow, drifting clouds, floating light, and two sword-bearing figures.
   The opening button is **I’ll draw my destiny**.
2. **Prologue:** two animated dialogue pages introduce Moss and the evil twin.
   The last button leads to login/sign-up. **Show all dialogue** skips the typing
   effect; it never skips the page itself.
3. **Login/sign-up:** an open book shows Moss and a story passage on the left,
   with Log in / Sign up on the right. Auth0 credentials open in a popup, keeping
   the book open behind it. Closing the window or choosing **Back to the book**
   leaves the player on the hero ledger. Successful authentication opens game selection. Signing out
   returns directly to the hero ledger. See [Auth0 appearance](auth0-appearance.md)
   for matching hosted-login colors and the optional back-button page template.
4. **Choose a game:** name your hero, create/join an online room, or train with Swamp Bot.
5. **Ready your sword:** after creating or joining a room (including Swamp Bot),
   the book opens **Wait! You need your sword!**. Both modes show the Pairing,
   Test Lights, Motion, and Camera checklist. **Calibrate practice sword** runs
   simulated checks; only after all four pass does the centered **Connect your sword**
   button unlock and open the lobby. Physical pairing and camera capture remain
   placeholders, with keyboard controls for battles. Preparation is kept during rematches
   in that room and resets when you leave; no quest can skip this page.
6. **Lobby:** see both players and the four-digit code. The host chooses **Start my quest**.
7. **Quest briefing:** Moss describes the evil twin’s latest mischief in fewer than
   50 words, generated by Gemini when configured or selected from local stories.
   **Accept my quest** begins combat after both online players have accepted.
   Swamp Bot is ready automatically and waits for the human player.
   Starting the quest closes the book directly into this briefing, without an
   extra Moss chat window or a chapter banner.
   Moss peeks around the scroll's lower-left corner with his speech bubble above
   him on the parchment, leaving the decorative knight unobstructed.
   Moss reads the script automatically. **Mute Moss** persists across pages and
   silences the voice while it continues. Next stops that page’s audio.
   ElevenLabs is the main voice,
   with browser text-to-speech as its backup. During
   battle, Moss can speak from short hit/miss/heal reaction banks. See
   [combat and voice](combat-and-voice.md) for sound controls and spell rules.
8. **Battle:** existing 3D sword combat with parchment health/spell panels, Moss's
   encouragement, and a looking-glass camera placeholder. Victory/defeat leads
   to rematch or leave. Rematch keeps the room, returns both players to its lobby,
   and lets them open a fresh briefing for the next round.

The sword wordmark and music controls float over the scene with no header strip.
In battle, the left column stacks health bars, Moss's voice switch, the game mode
and the Leave game button. Moss and his dialogue sit above the battle log, which
is anchored at the bottom left. The log scrolls as needed to keep messages from
overlapping the guide.
Spell instructions extend down from the upper-right corner to align with the
camera's bottom edge on wide screens. The looking-glass camera sits
along the bottom, to their left on wide screens and beneath them on smaller
screens. Moss has a warm parchment portrait
backdrop to stand out from the meadow.

## Edit the story

The fixed prologue lives in **`apps/web/src/story/chapters.ts`**. Quest briefings
arrive from the backend, shared by both players. See [Gemini setup](gemini-setup.md)
for model settings, credentials, usage limits, and hosting. Local fallback copy
lives in **`packages/protocol/src/quests.ts`**.

| File | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `apps/web/src/components/StoryScene.tsx` | Reusable dialogue layout and text reveal |
| `apps/web/src/components/PrologueScreen.tsx` | Opening story pages and login transition |
| `apps/web/src/components/QuestScreen.tsx` | Quest copy, acceptance, and waiting state |
| `apps/web/src/components/NpcGuide.tsx` | Moss artwork and short captions |
| `apps/web/src/components/WorldBackdrop.tsx` | Landscape, decorative fighters, and ambient animation |
| `apps/web/src/styles.css` | Shared theme, responsive layouts, reduced-motion styling |
| `apps/web/public/art/` | Original SVG meadow, Moss, and decorative knight illustrations |

Gemini generation and validation run on the backend. Generation starts in the
lobby; a bounded wait chooses a local story if needed. Both players receive
the same chosen story, and combat waits while they read. Google credentials
never enter Vite environment variables or the browser bundle.

Auth0 hosts the actual email/password form outside this repository. This update
themes the app's login/sign-up entry page and keeps the working Auth0 flow.
Auth0's own form branding is managed separately in its dashboard.

## Multiplayer behavior

`quest.begin` is host-only. It broadcasts `quest.state` to the room while the
database match remains waiting. Each player sends `quest.accept`; only when both
are connected and accepted does the server start the battle. Reconnecting during
the briefing restores its acceptance state. Leaving closes the room as before.
No database migration is needed for these UI/story states.

Both frontend and backend need this version of the shared protocol. Restart a
non-watching backend after updating it; Vite and `tsx watch` normally pick up
local source changes. Gemini is optional and requires backend environment and
ADC settings; the game keeps working with local stories without them.

## Check the flow

Open the local website, follow the prologue, and use guest Swamp Bot to try the
whole sequence without login. To verify online play, use two different accounts
in the same room: confirm that one acceptance does not start combat, then accept
on the second client. Check rematch, reconnect, and leaving as well.

From each app directory run `npm run check`, `npm test`, and `npm run build`.
The connection tests cover the bot's quest gate; backend tests exercise a real
HTTP/WebSocket quest flow with test identities, including reconnect and rematch.
Motion-sensitive players can use their system's reduced-motion preference to
disable decorative animation and display dialogue immediately.
